# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

**Teachers (~15):** Using the app daily on a phone, between lessons or right after a session. They need to log a student's progress in under 30 seconds, standing up, with one thumb. They are busy, trusted, and not tech-averse but not power users either.

**Parents and students (~350 families):** Checking progress occasionally — after a school event, before a parent-teacher meeting, or when a child mentions they moved to a new surah. They want clarity and reassurance, not dashboards.

**Admins (1–3):** Managing the school year cycle, importing student lists, overseeing all classes. They work seated, less frequently, but need confidence in the data.

## Product Purpose

Al Bayyinah School Progress System tracks Quranic learning (Iqro and Quran memorization) for approximately 350 students across 15 teachers. It replaces a paper → Excel → PDF macro workflow.

Success looks like: a teacher finishes a lesson, taps three times, and the parent can see their child's progress that afternoon — without anyone touching a spreadsheet.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy, Calm, Clear.

This is an institution tool, not a consumer app. It carries the weight of a school trusted with children's Quranic education. The interface should feel like a well-kept record kept by someone who cares — not a startup product, not a government form.

Reference feel: Quran.com and Tarteel — respectful of Arabic script, warm without being loud, typography-forward, spiritual weight without heaviness.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS dashboard** — No dark sidebars, no metric cards with gradient accents, no Linear/Notion aesthetic imported wholesale. This is not a productivity startup.
- **Government / bureaucratic** — No dense data tables as the primary UI, no form-heavy layouts, no sterile white-and-grey with no identity.
- **Edtech gamification** — No Duolingo-style XP bars, no confetti, no badge popups that trivialize the act of memorizing Quran.

## Design Principles

1. **Identity in every screen.** Even the most utilitarian progress-log form should feel unmistakably Al Bayyinah — not a white-label SaaS tool with the school's name swapped in.

2. **Spiritual weight, not decorative religion.** Arabic typography and Islamic visual vocabulary are structural, not ornamental. Amiri at generous size, RTL handled with precision, not as an afterthought.

3. **Calm before clever.** Teachers log progress dozens of times a day. The interface must never excite or alarm — it should steady. Color signals (green, amber, red) should whisper, not shout.

4. **Typography as hierarchy.** Montserrat for function, EB Garamond for warmth and headings. Let scale and weight build structure. Resist reaching for another card, another border, another badge.

5. **Three taps, then done.** Speed for teachers is a design constraint, not a nice-to-have. Every additional tap is a failure.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Minimum 44px touch targets on all interactive elements. Sufficient contrast for outdoor / low-light phone use. Arabic text must be marked with `lang="ar"` and `dir="rtl"` for screen reader correctness. No motion-critical interactions.
